# Market Analysis — Siebi s.r.o.

Internal document. Not published on the site. Grounded in public web research (July 2026) — treat as a directional starting point, not a substitute for primary research (talking to actual prospects, pricing competitors directly).

## 1. What business this actually is

Siebi s.r.o. does one thing: **sieťotlač (screen printing) directly onto non-standard, often industrial substrates** — metal, glass, plastic, self-adhesive foil — using two-component, chemically-cured inks. The flagship application is **ADR nálepky**: hazard-class labeling printed directly onto drums, tanks, and vehicle panels used to transport dangerous goods.

This is a narrow, technique-defined niche, not a general print shop. That narrowness is the strategy (per the site-scope decision already made) — the question this document answers is whether the niche is defensible and growing.

## 2. Competitive landscape

Two distinct competitor types showed up in research, and they are *not* the same threat everywhere:

**A. Generic ADR/pictogram sticker e-shops** — sell **pre-printed, standardized ADR pictograms as adhesive stickers**: catalog products, fast checkout, low price, presumably vinyl/digital print, not screen-printed-in inks. They compete on price and speed for buyers who just need a compliant, off-the-shelf label to stick on.

**B. Direct-print industrial screen printers** — companies capable of screen-printing durable ink *onto* metal drums, tanks, and panels rather than selling a sticker to apply afterward. This is Siebi's real competitive set, and it's a much smaller group everywhere.

**The actual differentiation**: a stuck-on adhesive sticker delamines under abrasion, chemical exposure, and repeated washing — exactly the conditions ADR-labeled drums and tanks face in industrial use. A screen-printed, two-component-ink mark is chemically bonded to the surface. That's a real, defensible, explainable difference — and it's the single strongest thing to lead with on the ADR page and in any sales conversation, because most of category A's competitors cannot make this claim.

### 2.1 Slovakia — deep pass

**Category A (sticker e-shops)**: Piktogram.sk, Moja Značka (mojaznacka.sk), Lepiacepasky.sk (Adhesive Tapes s.r.o.), Lacnenalepky.sk, Truckshop.sk / SlovakTruckShop.sk, Arcabox.eu, Safetyshop.sk, REO AMOS (reoamos.sk), Nalepkovna.sk. Real price anchor found: SlovakTruckShop.sk sells a reflective ADR "limited quantity" sticker at **€2.20/pc incl. VAT**, in-store pickup — this is the kind of price a confused buyer will compare Siebi against if the durability story isn't made obvious first.

**Category B (screen printers capable of metal/industrial work)**: **Sietotlac.sk** is the closest match found — operating since 1991 (a near-identical heritage story to Siebi's 1992 founding), doing screen printing on metal, glass, plastic, textile, paper. It's a *broader* general screen-printer, not ADR-focused, so it's more of an adjacent capability-overlap than a direct positioning competitor. No SK competitor was found that specifically markets "screen-printed ADR labels" as a distinct product the way DN Sign does in Germany (see 2.4) — that specific positioning looks genuinely open in Slovakia right now.

**Trade context**: FESPA SK (Slovenský zväz sieťotlačiarov a digitálnych tlačiarov, Siebi is a member) publishes a member list at szsdt.sk — worth a direct look when there's time, to see which other members do industrial/metal work near Piešťany/Trnava/Bratislava specifically.

### 2.2 Czech Republic — lighter pass

Category A/general sticker-and-screen-print shops: Hanák sítotisk, Tiskárna Adámek, SAMOLEPKY.CZ (explicitly screen-print onto PVC/PE/PP and electrostatic film), PPtisk.cz, Faltus (Uherské Hradiště). **ČESMAD BOHEMIA** (the Czech road-transport trade association) sells ADR samolepky directly to its transport-company membership via its webshop — a distribution channel worth noting, not a manufacturer. No CZ company was found marketing screen-printed-direct-to-drum ADR labeling as a specific service; the market reads the same shape as Slovakia's — general sticker/screen-print shops, nobody owning the "durable direct-print ADR" positioning specifically.

### 2.3 Austria — lighter pass

SIMSA (Vienna, sticker specialist since 1972), druck.wien (screen printing across paper/plastic/metal/glass/wood/textile, plus safety labeling), Eins Zwei Drei Druck (Tirol). General sticker/screen-print capability exists; again no company found specifically positioned around durable, direct-printed ADR/hazard labeling as its lead product.

### 2.4 Germany — lighter pass, most important finding

**DN Sign (dn-sign.de)** is the one real precedent found anywhere in this research for Siebi's exact positioning: they explicitly state their PVC-film hazard labels are **"produced using screen printing"** specifically because it makes them outdoor-durable, and they quote a temperature resistance spec (-40°C to +120°C) as a selling point, deliver DE/AT/CH three times a week, and explicitly support small custom-quantity orders. This is proof the "screen-printed, durability-led ADR label" positioning is a real, sellable category — not a story Siebi invented. It's also a template worth studying for how to phrase the durability claim concretely (a temperature spec reads more credible than "resistant to weather").

Other DE players are mostly category A at scale: GARREIS, Wolski Etiketten, VT-Etiketten (flexo/digital/screen/letterpress/offset combined), HERMA, DENIOS, Kroschke SIGN, Transpack Krumbach — large, established label suppliers selling ADR-compliant labels as one SKU among thousands, not a specialist story.

**Takeaway across all four countries**: nobody in SK/CZ/AT has claimed the "durable, screen-printed, direct-onto-metal ADR label" positioning explicitly — DN Sign in Germany is the only found precedent, and it's evidence the positioning works, not evidence Siebi would be competing against it locally (different country, industrial-scale operation, not obviously reachable/threatening to a small Piešťany workshop). This makes the sk/cz/at markets look genuinely open for this specific message, not just "less researched."

## 3. Demand signal

- Global label market: ~3.28% CAGR (2026–2031, USD 55B → 67B). Growth is concentrated in **specialty/industrial labels**, not commoditized signage/apparel — commoditized segments are losing share to digital substitution, specialty/functional print is not. Siebi's niche sits on the resilient side of that split.
- Slovakia-specific driver for 2026: **stricter dangerous-waste/battery transport rules** are being phased in (e.g. lithium battery waste reclassification, vyhláška 89/2024 effective 2027), driven by growth in EV/battery/e-mobility waste streams. This is a genuine near-term tailwind for ADR compliance demand — companies newly subject to ADR labeling requirements are new prospects, not just existing shippers renewing labels.
- ADR itself is EU-wide and language-agnostic in its symbols (the hazard diamonds are standardized), which is exactly why the sk/en/de site makes sense — German/Austrian logistics and chemical companies are a real addressable market, not a stretch.

## 4. Customer segments (inferred, not yet validated)

1. **Chemical/industrial manufacturers** shipping their own product in drums/tanks and needing compliant, durable labeling on the container itself (Siebi's existing clients — Chirana Dental, EKOM — likely fall here or adjacent).
2. **Logistics/transport companies** needing vehicle-level ADR marking (panels, tabule) that survives years of road use, washing, weather.
3. **Waste management / battery-recycling operators** — the emerging segment given the 2026–2027 regulatory tailwind above.

Segment 3 is worth validating directly (a few outreach calls) before betting marketing spend on it — the regulatory signal is real but Siebi's current client list doesn't obviously include this segment yet.

## 5. Risks

- **Category A price competition on perception**: a buyer who doesn't understand the delamination/durability difference will price-compare Siebi against a €2.20 adhesive sticker (real price found at SlovakTruckShop.sk) and walk away confused. The site's job is to make that distinction obvious before price ever comes up — this is why the ADR page needs to explain *why* screen printing rather than just listing it as a feature.
- **Small operation, capacity ceiling**: nothing in research suggests Siebi is more than a small team; this is a strength for quality/craft positioning but a real constraint if the regulatory tailwind in segment 3 produces a demand spike.
- **No visible online competitor owning category B's exact positioning in SK/CZ/AT** doesn't mean no competitor exists — it means this document's research method (public web search) can't see them. Treat the "no direct competitor found" finding as *not yet disproven*, not as *confirmed absence*. Germany's DN Sign (§2.4) proves the category is real and reachable by search, which makes its absence elsewhere more notable but still not conclusive.
